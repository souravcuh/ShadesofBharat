ऋषि परंपरा

Rishi Parampara

From the Saptarishi who are said to have seen the Vedic hymns, through the sages of the Ramayana and Mahabharata, to the philosopher-acharyas and the poet-saints of the Bhaktmal — an unbroken chain of teachers carrying the tradition forward. Tap any card below for the fuller story.

सप्तर्षि

The Saptarishi — Seven Great Seers

The Puranas name seven great rishis, born of Brahma's mind, credited as the seers behind much of the Rigveda's hymns and, in several cases, as direct guides and kinsmen to the heroes of the epics.

वशिष्ठ
Vashishtha
सप्तर्षि · Saptarishi

One of the Saptarishi and family guru (kulaguru) of the Raghu dynasty — the sage who guided Dasharatha's court and later tutored Rama himself.

विश्वामित्र
Vishwamitra
सप्तर्षि · Saptarishi

A king who became a sage through fierce tapasya — traditionally credited with the Gayatri Mantra, and Rama's guide for part of the Ramayana.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
कश्यप
Kashyapa
सप्तर्षि · Saptarishi

Held in the Puranas as father to gods, demons, and much of creation itself — one of the Saptarishi and a name behind Kashmir's own.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
अत्रि
Atri
सप्तर्षि · Saptarishi

One of the Saptarishi, husband of Anasuya — the couple whose extraordinary devotion is held to have given rise to Dattatreya.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
भरद्वाज
Bharadwaja
सप्तर्षि · Saptarishi

One of the Saptarishi, and the sage whose forest hermitage on the Ganga hosted Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana at the start of their exile.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
गौतम ऋषि
Gautama Rishi
सप्तर्षि · Saptarishi

One of the Saptarishi — husband of Ahalya, whose story of a curse and eventual liberation by Rama is among the Ramayana's most retold episodes.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
जमदग्नि
Jamadagni
सप्तर्षि · Saptarishi

One of the Saptarishi, and father of Parashurama, the axe-wielding sixth avatar of Vishnu.

वेद-शास्त्र रचयिता

Compiler-Sages — Who Gave the Tradition Its Texts

A handful of sages are credited with the texts this entire site draws on: the Vedas, the Ramayana, the Yoga Sutras, and the grammar that has held classical Sanskrit together for over two thousand years.

वेद व्यास
Ved Vyasa
द्वापर युग · Dvapara Yuga

Traditionally credited with compiling the four Vedas, composing the Mahabharata, and authoring the Puranas — perhaps the single most prolific figure in the entire tradition.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
वाल्मीकि
Valmiki
त्रेता युग · Treta Yuga

Author of the Ramayana, the itihasa this site's Itihasa chapter draws on — traditionally a hunter-turned-sage transformed by remorse and meditation.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
पतंजलि
Patanjali
लगभग २री शताब्दी ई.पू. · circa 2nd century BCE

Compiler of the Yoga Sutras, the foundational text systematizing yoga's eight limbs (ashtanga) into a coherent philosophical path.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
पाणिनि
Panini
लगभग ४–६ठी शताब्दी ई.पू. · circa 4th-6th century BCE

Author of the Ashtadhyayi, a grammar of Sanskrit so precise it is still studied as a landmark in linguistics, roughly 2,500 years after it was written.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
देव-ऋषि व दिव्य ऋषि

Divine Sages — Between Gods and Mortals

Neither fully divine nor fully human, these sages move through the Puranas, Ramayana, and Mahabharata as messengers, teachers, and occasionally as the trigger for a story's entire plot.

नारद
Narada
देव-ऋषि · Deva-rishi

The wandering deva-rishi, veena in hand, who appears across the Puranas, Ramayana, and Mahabharata as messenger, mischief-maker, and teacher of devotion.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
दत्तात्रेय
Dattatreya
दिव्य ऋषि · Divine Sage

Held as a combined incarnation of Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva together — a teacher said to have learned from twenty-four different gurus, including animals and natural elements.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
अगस्त्य
Agastya
दिव्य ऋषि · Divine Sage

A sage credited with carrying Vedic and Shaiva tradition into South India, and with drinking the ocean dry in one Puranic tale.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
दुर्वासा
Durvasa
दिव्य ऋषि · Divine Sage

A sage famed, and feared, for a temper quick to curse, appearing at pivotal moments across the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Bhagavata Purana.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
मार्कण्डेय
Markandeya
दिव्य ऋषि · Divine Sage

A sage granted eternal youth, whose vision of the cosmic dissolution and rebirth is recounted directly to Yudhishthira in the Mahabharata.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
आचार्य, नीतिज्ञ व वैज्ञानिक

Acharyas, Statesmen & Scholars

Historical figures whose written work still shapes how the tradition is studied, governed, and understood today — from temple philosophy to statecraft to the mathematics of the stars.

चाणक्य
Chanakya
लगभग ४थी शताब्दी ई.पू. · circa 4th century BCE

Strategist and statesman, also known as Kautilya — the mind behind the Mauryan Empire's rise and author of the Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft still studied today.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
आदि शंकराचार्य
Adi Shankaracharya
८वीं शताब्दी · 8th century CE

The philosopher already met throughout this site's Tirtha chapter — founder of the Char Dham and the chief systematizer of Advaita Vedanta, non-dual philosophy.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
रामानुजाचार्य
Ramanujacharya
११वीं–१२वीं शताब्दी · 11th-12th century CE

The philosopher-saint who articulated Vishishtadvaita, qualified non-dualism, and reshaped Vaishnava devotional practice across South India.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
मध्वाचार्य
Madhvacharya
१३वीं शताब्दी · 13th century CE

The philosopher-saint of Udupi who founded Dvaita Vedanta, arguing for an eternal, real distinction between the soul and the divine.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
आर्यभट
Aryabhata
जन्म ४७६ ई. · born 476 CE

The mathematician-astronomer whose Aryabhatiya calculated the length of a year, explained eclipses, and treated the earth as rotating on its axis — centuries ahead of similar ideas elsewhere.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
भक्तमाल के संत

Saints of the Bhaktmal — the Bhakti Movement

Nabhadas's 17th-century Bhaktmal catalogued the devotional saints of the centuries before it — poets from every region and, often, every caste background, who sang devotion directly rather than through ritual alone. A selection follows.

तुलसीदास
Tulsidas
१६वीं शताब्दी · 16th century CE

Author of the Ramcharitmanas, the Awadhi retelling of the Ramayana that remains the most widely read version of Rama's story across North India today.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
कबीर
Kabir
१५वीं शताब्दी · 15th century CE

A poet-saint whose blunt, often provocative dohas challenged caste, ritual, and religious division alike, claimed today by Hindu and Sufi traditions both.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
सूरदास
Surdas
१६वीं शताब्दी · 16th century CE

A blind poet-saint whose Krishna bhakti poetry, the Sursagar, is counted among the richest devotional literature dedicated to Krishna's childhood.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
मीराबाई
Mirabai
१६वीं शताब्दी · 16th century CE

The Rajput princess-turned-saint whose devotional songs to Krishna, sung in defiance of her royal family's objections, remain among the most beloved Krishna bhakti poetry in India.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
रविदास
Ravidas
१५वीं शताब्दी · 15th century CE

A cobbler-saint whose bhakti poetry rejected caste hierarchy outright, still revered widely today, including within the Guru Granth Sahib.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
नामदेव
Namdev
१३वीं शताब्दी · 13th century CE

A Maharashtrian tailor-saint whose kirtan-centered devotion to Vithoba helped shape the Varkari bhakti tradition of Maharashtra.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
चैतन्य महाप्रभु
Chaitanya Mahaprabhu
जन्म १४८६ ई. · born 1486 CE

The Bengali saint whose ecstatic public chanting of Krishna's names ignited the Gaudiya Vaishnava bhakti movement, later carried worldwide through ISKCON.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
तुकाराम
Tukaram
१७वीं शताब्दी · 17th century CE

A Maharashtrian saint-poet whose abhangs to Vithoba remain central to the Varkari tradition's devotional repertoire alongside Namdev's.
