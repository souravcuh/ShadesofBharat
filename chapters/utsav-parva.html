उत्सव एवं पर्व

Utsav & Parva — Festivals

The Hindu festival year, told from its very first day — Navavarsha, the new year that arrives on different dates in different regions, all rooted in the Panchang.

भारत में नववर्ष एक तिथि नहीं, अनेक हैं — क्योंकि पंचांग स्वयं एक नहीं है। In Bharat, the new year isn't a single date, because the Panchang — the traditional almanac by which festival dates are set — isn't a single system. Most of the subcontinent follows a lunisolar Panchang, where the year turns on Chaitra Shukla Pratipada, the first day of the moon's bright fortnight in the month of Chaitra — a date that shifts each year against the Gregorian calendar. Much of the east, south, and northwest instead follow a solar Panchang, where the year turns on Mesha Sankranti, the sun's entry into Aries — a fixed date, usually 13–15 April. Same season, two different clocks, one shared spirit of renewal.

चान्द्र-सौर नववर्ष

Lunisolar New Year — Chaitra Shukla Pratipada

गुड़ी पड़वा
Gudi Padwa
महाराष्ट्र, कोंकण

Marks the Hindu new year and the start of the Shaka Samvat era. Homes raise a gudi — a bright cloth on a pole topped with an upturned kalash and neem leaves — outside the door. Tradition holds this as the day Brahma began creation, and the day Shalivahana's legendary victory began the Shaka calendar.

उगादि
Ugadi
आंध्र प्रदेश, तेलंगाना, कर्नाटक

"Yuga-adi" — the start of an age. Families read the year's forecast from the new Panchang and share ugadi pachadi, a dish blending six tastes to mark that the year ahead, like life, carries sweetness, bitterness, and everything between.

चेटी चंड
Cheti Chand
सिंधी समुदाय

The birth anniversary of Jhulelal, revered by the Sindhi community as an incarnation of Varuna, lord of the waters — celebrated with river processions and water rituals.

सौर नववर्ष

Solar New Year — Mesha Sankranti

विशु
Vishu
केरल

The day opens with vishukkani — the first sight arranged the night before: rice, fruit, flowers, gold, and a mirror, believed to shape the fortune of the year ahead.

पुत्तांडु
Puthandu
तमिलनाडु

The Tamil solar new year. Homes prepare a kani — an auspicious first sight of the year — visit temples, and traders open fresh account books.

पोइला बैशाख
Poila Boishakh
पश्चिम बंगाल

Marked by Halkhata — merchants close the old year's ledger and open a new one, invoking Ganesha and Lakshmi over sweets shared with customers.

बोहाग बिहू
Bohag Bihu (Rongali)
असम

Assam's new year opens the sowing season. Goru Bihu, the day before, honors cattle with a bath and garlands; the days that follow bring feasting and the Bihu dance.

बैसाखी
Baisakhi
पंजाब

Marks the rabi harvest and the solar new year across Punjab, celebrated with bhangra, gidda, and fairs in the fields.

चैत्र शुक्ल १–९

Chaitra Navratri & Ram Navami

चैत्र नवरात्रि
Chaitra Navratri
चैत्र शुक्ल प्रतिपदा – नवमी

Nine nights of worship to the Devi in her nine forms (Navadurga), kept with fasting and evening aarti. Bengal calls this Vasanti Puja — the original seasonal Durga worship, distinct from the larger autumn Durga Puja, which legend traces to an akal bodhan, an untimely invocation Rama himself is said to have performed before his battle with Ravana.

राम नवमी
Ram Navami
चैत्र शुक्ल नवमी

The ninth and closing day of Chaitra Navratri, marking the birth of Rama — prince of Ayodhya and hero of the Ramayana — to King Dasharatha and Queen Kausalya at noon. Temples hold readings of the Ramcharitmanas and a cradle ceremony for the infant Rama.

वैशाख शुक्ल तृतीया

Akshaya Tritiya & Parashurama Jayanti

अक्षय तृतीया
Akshaya Tritiya
वैशाख शुक्ल तृतीया

"The imperishable third" — a day considered so auspicious that no muhurta (astrological timing) is needed to begin something on it; anything started today, tradition holds, endures. Marked by buying gold and opening new ventures, and by the ceremonial opening of the Kedarnath temple doors for the pilgrimage season.

परशुराम जयंती
Parashurama Jayanti
वैशाख शुक्ल तृतीया

The birth of Parashurama, sixth avatar of Vishnu — a sage-warrior who wields an axe (parashu) gifted by Shiva, and who appears in both the Ramayana and Mahabharata as a bridge between the two epics' eras.

आषाढ़

Ashadha — Rath Yatra & Guru Purnima

रथ यात्रा
Rath Yatra
आषाढ़ शुक्ल द्वितीया · पुरी, ओडिशा

Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra are drawn through the streets of Puri in immense wooden chariots, from the Jagannath Temple to the Gundicha Temple. Devotees of every background pull the ropes together — historically one of the few times the deities left the temple to be seen by all, regardless of who was normally allowed inside.

गुरु पूर्णिमा
Guru Purnima
आषाढ़ पूर्णिमा

Also Vyasa Purnima — the birth anniversary of Ved Vyasa, who compiled the Vedas and composed the Mahabharata and the Puranas. Students offer respect to their teachers on this day, honoring the guru as the one who removes the darkness of ignorance.

श्रावण

Shravan — Raksha Bandhan & Nag Panchami

रक्षाबंधन
Raksha Bandhan
श्रावण पूर्णिमा

A sister ties a rakhi — a protective thread — on her brother's wrist, and he promises to look out for her in return. The bond isn't limited to blood siblings; the thread has historically marked any relationship of protection, including between communities and, in some retellings, between rulers and allies.

नाग पंचमी
Nag Panchami
श्रावण शुक्ल पंचमी

A day of reverence for serpents (nagas), honored as guardians of water, fertility, and the underworld — offerings of milk are made at snake shrines and anthills, and Shiva is worshipped with the serpent Vasuki coiled at his neck in mind.

भाद्रपद

Bhadrapada — Janmashtami & Ganesh Chaturthi

जन्माष्टमी
Janmashtami
भाद्रपद कृष्ण अष्टमी

The midnight birth of Krishna in a prison cell in Mathura, to Devaki and Vasudeva, under a sky the legend says was thrown into darkness and rain so his father could carry him to safety across the Yamuna. Marked with fasting until midnight, cradle ceremonies, and, in Maharashtra, the dahi handi — human towers built to break a pot of curd hung out of reach, re-enacting young Krishna's mischief.

गणेश चतुर्थी
Ganesh Chaturthi
भाद्रपद शुक्ल चतुर्थी

Clay images of Ganesha, remover of obstacles, are installed in homes and public pandals for anywhere from one and a half to eleven days, worshipped daily, and then carried in procession to a river, tank, or the sea for visarjan — immersion, sending the deity home again. Maharashtra's public celebrations, revived by Bal Gangadhar Tilak in 1893 as a gathering point during colonial rule, are now among the largest in the country.

आश्विन

Ashwin — Sharad Navratri, Durga Puja & Dussehra

शरद नवरात्रि
Sharad Navratri
आश्विन शुक्ल १–९

The larger of the year's two widely-observed Navratris — nine nights honoring the Devi through her nine forms, from Shailaputri on the first night to Siddhidatri on the ninth. Garba and dandiya raas fill courtyards across Gujarat each evening; elsewhere the fast is kept quietly at home.

दुर्गा पूजा
Durga Puja
आश्विन शुक्ल षष्ठी–दशमी

Bengal's grandest festival: elaborate pandals house clay images of Durga slaying the buffalo-demon Mahishasura, flanked by Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha, and Kartikeya. Five days of ritual run from Shashthi's unveiling to Dashami's tearful visarjan, when the goddess is carried to the river and asked to return next year.

दशहरा · विजयादशमी
Dussehra (Vijayadashami)
आश्विन शुक्ल दशमी

The tenth day closes both Navratris' stories at once: Durga's victory over Mahishasura, and Rama's victory over Ravana in the Ramayana, retold nightly through the Ram Lila and ended by burning towering effigies of Ravana, his brother Kumbhakarna, and his son Meghnad.

कार्तिक

Kartik — Diwali, Five Days

धनतेरस
Dhanteras
कार्तिक कृष्ण त्रयोदशी · दिन १

Honors Dhanvantari, the physician-deity said to have emerged from the churning of the cosmic ocean bearing the nectar of immortality. A day for buying gold, silver, or new utensils — the purchase itself is considered to invite prosperity into the year ahead.

नरक चतुर्दशी
Naraka Chaturdashi
कार्तिक कृष्ण चतुर्दशी · दिन २

Also Chhoti Diwali — marks Krishna's defeat of the demon Narakasura, who had held thousands captive. An early bath before sunrise is kept to wash away impurity ahead of the main night.

दिवाली · लक्ष्मी पूजा
Diwali (Lakshmi Puja)
कार्तिक अमावस्या · दिन ३

The main night — deepavali, "a row of lamps." Homes are lined with diyas to welcome Lakshmi, goddess of wealth, and to mark Rama's return to Ayodhya after fourteen years of exile and his victory over Ravana in the Ramayana; legend holds the city lit lamps to guide him home in the dark.

गोवर्धन पूजा
Govardhan Puja
कार्तिक शुक्ल प्रतिपदा · दिन ४

Recalls Krishna lifting the Govardhan hill on a single finger, sheltering the people of Vraj from the storm Indra sent after they turned their worship to Krishna instead. Households prepare annakut — a "mountain of food" — in offering.

भाई दूज
Bhai Dooj
कार्तिक शुक्ल द्वितीया · दिन ५

Closes the five days on the same note as Raksha Bandhan — a sister marks her brother's forehead with tilak and prays for his long life. Tradition traces it to Yama, the god of death, visiting his sister Yamuna, who welcomed him with exactly this ritual.
