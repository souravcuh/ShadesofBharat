Dharma & Shastra

Veda, Upanishad, Manusmriti, Varnashram, the slokas that carry them — and the granthas, pandulipis, and scripts that preserved them. Tap any card below for the fuller story.

वेद व पुराण

Vedas & Puranas

The oldest layer of the tradition's textual inheritance, shruti and smriti — already introduced in this site's Gyan-Vigyan chapter, and the natural starting point here too.

चार वेद
The Four Vedas
वैदिक काल · Vedic era

The oldest layer of the tradition's textual inheritance, Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda, and Atharvaveda, traditionally held as apaurusheya, without human author, heard rather than composed.

अठारह पुराण
The Eighteen Puranas
पौराणिक काल · Puranic era

Eighteen major Puranas, traditionally compiled by Ved Vyasa, weaving cosmology, mythology, genealogy, and devotion into the stories most Hindus actually grow up hearing.

उपनिषद

The Upanishads

The philosophical closing chapters embedded within each Veda, asking what underlies the ritual world rather than how to perform it. Five of the principal Upanishads follow; two more, Brihadaranyaka and Chandogya, are already told through the sages who appear within them, Yajnavalkya, Gargi, Maitreyi, and Uddalaka Aruni, in this site's Rishi Parampara chapter.

ईश उपनिषद्
Isha Upanishad
शुक्ल यजुर्वेद के अंतर्गत · within the Shukla Yajurveda

The shortest of the principal Upanishads, opening the Shukla Yajurveda with a single teaching: that the divine pervades everything, and true renunciation means enjoying the world without grasping at it.

कठ उपनिषद्
Katha Upanishad
कृष्ण यजुर्वेद के अंतर्गत · within the Krishna Yajurveda

The dialogue between the boy Nachiketa and Yama, lord of death, on the nature of the Self — one of the tradition's most vivid and directly narrative philosophical texts.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
केन उपनिषद्
Kena Upanishad
सामवेद के अंतर्गत · within the Samaveda

'By whom' — an inquiry into the power behind mind, speech, and the senses, answered through the story of gods humbled by an unrecognized power they cannot name.

मुण्डक उपनिषद्
Mundaka Upanishad
अथर्ववेद के अंतर्गत · within the Atharvaveda

Source of 'Satyameva Jayate,' truth alone triumphs, the phrase inscribed beneath India's national emblem — and home to the parable of two birds on one tree.

माण्डूक्य उपनिषद्
Mandukya Upanishad
अथर्ववेद के अंतर्गत · within the Atharvaveda

The shortest of all the principal Upanishads, just twelve verses, entirely devoted to analyzing the syllable Om and four states of consciousness.

धर्मशास्त्र

Dharmashastra

The tradition's law-and-conduct literature — texts on duty, governance, and social order, of which the Manusmriti is the best known and the most debated.

मनुस्मृति
Manusmriti
लगभग २री शताब्दी ई.पू. – २री शताब्दी · circa 2nd century BCE - 2nd century CE

One of the tradition's most influential, and most contested, Dharmashastra texts, covering law, conduct, and social order — a work whose legacy is argued over as much today as its content.

वर्णाश्रम

Varnashram

The social and life-stage framework the Manusmriti and other Dharmashastra texts describe — covered here on its own, since its later, rigid history is a large enough subject to warrant separate treatment.

वर्णाश्रम
Varnashram
वैदिक व स्मृति साहित्य · Vedic & Smriti literature

The two-part framework of varna, four social categories, and ashrama, four life stages — a schema much debated in its later, rigid form, but originally framed around guna (quality) and karma (action) rather than birth alone.

लिपियाँ व पांडुलिपियाँ

Scripts & Manuscripts

None of the above survives without a physical form. This is the part of the story about how these texts were actually written down and kept alive across centuries.

ब्राह्मी लिपि
Brahmi Script
लगभग ३री शताब्दी ई.पू. से · attested from circa 3rd century BCE

The ancestral script behind nearly every writing system native to the Indian subcontinent — the script Ashoka's edicts were carved in, and the root from which Devanagari itself descended.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
देवनागरी लिपि
Devanagari Script
वर्तमान रूप लगभग ११–१३वीं शताब्दी · present form circa 11th-13th century CE

The script this very site's Hindi and Sanskrit text is printed in — 'the writing of the city of the gods,' refined from Brahmi's descendants to become the primary script for Sanskrit, Hindi, Marathi, and Nepali today.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
ताड़पत्र पांडुलिपियाँ
Palm-Leaf Manuscripts (Tadapatra)
शताब्दियों तक प्रयुक्त · used across centuries

The physical medium that carried Sanskrit literature across two thousand years before paper and printing — dried palm leaves, inscribed with a stylus and rubbed with ink, bundled and stored for centuries at a time.
