Kala & Nirman

Dance, handicraft, and design alongside timeless art — and the innovation that keeps renewing it. This chapter starts with the handcrafts themselves: what is made, from what material, by whose hands, and where each tradition is still practiced today, then gathers that same craft heritage state by state, and names the artists behind it. Tap any card below for the fuller story.

कलाकार

Artists

Behind every craft tradition stand the individual artists who carried it, revived it, or gave it a form the rest of the country came to recognize. This section will grow — each artist, like each craft, has a history worth its own page.

राजा रवि वर्मा
Raja Ravi Varma
केरल · Kerala, 1848–1906

A painter from the princely family of Kilimanoor who joined European oil-painting technique to Indian mythological subject matter, and whose images of the gods, reproduced by the thousand through his own Bombay lithography press, became the visual template millions of households across the country still recognize today.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
जामिनी रॉय
Jamini Roy
पश्चिम बंगाल · West Bengal, 1887–1972

A Bengal painter trained in the European academic style at Calcutta's Government College of Art who deliberately turned away from it in his thirties, toward the flat lines, bold colour, and folk subject matter of Bengal's own patua scroll painters — building, from that turn, one of the most recognizable and widely imitated visual styles in modern Indian art.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
अवनींद्रनाथ ठाकुर
Abanindranath Tagore
पश्चिम बंगाल · West Bengal, 1871–1951

A nephew of Rabindranath Tagore who founded the Bengal School of Art at the turn of the twentieth century, deliberately drawing on Mughal and Rajput miniature painting and Ajanta mural technique rather than the European academic style then taught in Indian art schools, and whose most famous single painting, Bharat Mata, gave the nation its enduring visual personification as a goddess-like figure.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
जनगढ़ सिंह श्याम
Jangarh Singh Shyam
मध्य प्रदेश · Madhya Pradesh, 1962–2001

A Pardhan Gond artist from a village near Patangarh in Madhya Pradesh who, working first with brush and paper and then canvas rather than the mud-and-wall surfaces his community's ritual art had traditionally used, single-handedly opened what is now known across India and internationally as contemporary Gond art.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
जिव्या सोमा माशे
Jivya Soma Mashe
महाराष्ट्र · Maharashtra, 1934–2018

A Warli artist from a village in Maharashtra's Thane district who was the first to take the community's ritual wall painting, traditionally made only by married women for marriage ceremonies, onto paper and canvas as an individually authored work of art, and in doing so carried Warli painting from a single region's ritual practice to recognition across India and abroad.
