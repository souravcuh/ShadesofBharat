मनुस्मृति

Manusmriti

One of the tradition's most influential Dharmashastra texts, covering law, conduct, and social order, and a work whose reception has shifted a great deal across the centuries since it was written.

लगभग २री शताब्दी ई.पू. – २री शताब्दी · circa 2nd century BCE - 2nd century CE

Dharmashastra
विषय-वस्तु व संरचना

Content & Structure

Composed in verse across twelve chapters, the Manusmriti addresses creation, the duties of the four varnas and four ashramas (life stages), civil and criminal law, and the conduct expected of a king, presenting itself as the teaching of Manu, the first human and progenitor of humankind in Puranic tradition. It is one of several Dharmashastra texts rather than the sole authority on any of these subjects within the tradition itself.

Reception Through History

The text's reception has varied widely by era and by reader. British colonial administrators, beginning with William Jones's 1794 translation, selected it among several competing Dharmashastra texts as a primary reference for codifying Hindu personal law under colonial rule, a role it had not held in quite the same way before. Readers continue to draw differing conclusions from the text today, as they do from law-and-conduct scripture in many traditions worldwide; this page presents the historical record and leaves the reader to weigh it.
