युधिष्ठिर

Yudhishthira

The eldest Pandava, son of the god Dharma himself, whose devotion to truth and duty is tested as severely as it is upheld across the Mahabharata.

महाभारत · Mahabharata

Mahabharata
कथा व भूमिका

Story & Role

Known for never speaking a lie, Yudhishthira's one recorded departure from strict truth, an ambiguous statement about an elephant named Ashwatthama during the war, intended to demoralize his teacher Drona, is treated in the epic itself as a serious moral compromise rather than a clean victory. He also loses his brothers, Draupadi, and the Pandava kingdom itself in a rigged game of dice against Shakuni, precipitating their thirteen years of exile.

महत्त्व

Significance

After the war, Yudhishthira rules as king before eventually renouncing the throne; the epic's closing books describe him ascending toward heaven still accompanied by a loyal dog, refusing to abandon it even at heaven's gate — a final test of character the Mahabharata frames as harder than any battle he fought.
