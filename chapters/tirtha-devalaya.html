तीर्थ एवं देवालय

Tirtha & Devalaya

A tirtha is literally a "crossing place" — a point where the sacred is close enough to reach. Across the subcontinent these crossings cluster into a few great circuits: the Char Dham, the Jyotirlingas, and the Shakti Peethas. This page walks through the first steps of each.

चतुर्दिशाओं का तीर्थ

The Char Dham — Pilgrimage of the Four Directions

Tradition credits the 8th-century philosopher Adi Shankaracharya with founding four abodes at the far corners of the subcontinent — one each in the north, south, east, and west — so that a single pilgrimage would carry a devotee across the whole of Bharat.

बद्रीनाथ
Badrinath
उत्तराखंड · उत्तर

Vishnu as Badrinarayan, seated on the Alaknanda's bank between the Nar and Narayan ranges — the northern anchor of the Char Dham, and shared with the Himalayan circuit below.

द्वारका
Dwarka
गुजरात · पश्चिम

Krishna's coastal capital on the Arabian Sea. The Dwarkadhish temple, its spire visible for miles, marks the western point of the circuit.

पुरी
Puri
ओडिशा · पूर्व

Home of Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra — wooden deities carved anew every twelve years or so — and of the Rath Yatra, when their chariots are pulled through the streets by the crowd itself.

रामेश्वरम्
Rameswaram
तमिलनाडु · दक्षिण

Tradition holds that Rama worshipped Shiva here before crossing to Lanka. The Ramanathaswamy temple is also counted among the twelve Jyotirlingas below — one site belonging to two circuits.

हिमालय की चार धाम यात्रा

The Himalayan Char Dham — Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath, Badrinath

A separate, smaller circuit set entirely in Uttarakhand's high Himalaya — open only for the months between snowmelt and the next winter's close. Not to be confused with the pan-India Char Dham above, though Badrinath belongs to both.

यमुनोत्री
Yamunotri
उत्तराखंड

The source shrine of the Yamuna, set beside a glacier at over 3,200 m — the westernmost and usually the first stop on the Himalayan circuit.

गंगोत्री
Gangotri
उत्तराखंड

The temple town for the Ganga, here still the Bhagirathi. The river's actual glacial source, Gaumukh, lies a trek further upstream.

केदारनाथ
Kedarnath
उत्तराखंड

Shiva at the head of the Mandakini valley, roughly 3,580 m up — also one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, and among the most physically demanding sites to reach on foot.

बद्रीनाथ
Badrinath
उत्तराखंड

The circuit's final and highest stop, closing the loop with the same shrine that opens the pan-India Char Dham above.

द्वादश ज्योतिर्लिंग

The Twelve Jyotirlingas

A jyotirlinga is a "linga of light" — a site where Shiva is held to have appeared as a self-manifest column of radiance rather than an image installed by human hands. Tradition counts twelve across the subcontinent; six of the twelve follow, the rest still to come.

सोमनाथ
Somnath
गुजरात

Reckoned first among the twelve, on the Arabian Sea coast. Destroyed and rebuilt repeatedly across the centuries, most recently after 1951.

काशी विश्वनाथ
Kashi Vishwanath
वाराणसी, उत्तर प्रदेश

On the Ganga's western bank in Varanasi — the city held to be Shiva's own, one he is said never to leave.

महाकालेश्वर
Mahakaleshwar
उज्जैन, मध्य प्रदेश

The only one of the twelve facing south. Known for the Bhasma Aarti, performed with sacred ash before sunrise each day.

त्र्यंबकेश्वर
Trimbakeshwar
नासिक, महाराष्ट्र

Near the source of the Godavari, one of the peninsula's great rivers — the temple's linga is unusually said to carry three faces within it.

वैद्यनाथ
Vaidyanath
देवघर, झारखंड

"The physician lord" — Shiva here in his aspect associated with healing, at the center of the Deoghar temple town.

घृष्णेश्वर
Grishneshwar
औरंगाबाद, महाराष्ट्र

Traditionally counted as the twelfth and last, just beside the Ellora cave temples cut into the same hillside.

शक्ति पीठ

The Shakti Peethas

Legend holds that when Shiva carried Sati's body in grief, Vishnu's discus cut it apart, and each place a piece fell became a Shakti Peetha — a site where the Devi herself, not an image of her, is held to reside. Traditional counts range from 51 to 108; four widely visited ones follow.

कामाख्या
Kamakhya
गुवाहाटी, असम

On Nilachal Hill above the Brahmaputra — one of the most important Shakti Peethas, and the center of the annual Ambubachi Mela.

कालीघाट
Kalighat
कोलकाता, पश्चिम बंगाल

Kali, on the Hooghly's bank — the temple many hold gave Kolkata its name, from "Kali" and "ghat," the riverside steps beside it.

ज्वालामुखी
Jwalamukhi
हिमाचल प्रदेश

The goddess worshipped as an eternal flame burning directly from the rock — no idol stands in the sanctum, only the fire itself.

अंबाजी
Ambaji
बनासकांठा, गुजरात

Near the Gujarat–Rajasthan border. Here too the sanctum holds no idol — worship centers on a yantra, a geometric diagram, in its place.

This chapter is being written further — the remaining six Jyotirlingas, dozens more Shakti Peethas, and temples like Tirupati, Meenakshi Madurai, Konark, and Khajuraho are still to come.
