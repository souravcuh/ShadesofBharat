बद्रीनाथ

Badrinath

Vishnu as Badrinarayan, seated on the Alaknanda's bank between the Nar and Narayan ranges — the northern anchor of the pan-India Char Dham, and the closing stop of the Himalayan circuit.

उत्तराखंड · Uttarakhand  —  विष्णु · Vishnu

Char DhamHimalayan Char Dham
पौराणिक कथा

Legend & Significance

Puranic tradition holds that Vishnu sat here in meditation for so long that Lakshmi took the form of a badri (Indian jujube) tree to shade him from the sun — giving the site its name, "Lord of the Badri." The valley is also linked to the sage-pair Nara-Narayana, said to have performed austerities on the twin peaks that flank the temple.

मंदिर

The Temple

The temple sits at roughly 3,100 m, open only from late April/May to November before Himalayan snow closes the valley for winter. Adi Shankaracharya is credited with reviving worship here in the 8th century, and the site has been part of both the pan-India Char Dham and the Himalayan Char Dham ever since — one shrine, two circuits.
