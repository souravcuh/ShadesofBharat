Itihasa — The Epics

"Thus it was" — itihasa is history carried as teaching. The Ramayana and the Mahabharata have shaped Bharat's sense of duty, family, and justice for millennia, retold in every language and every generation. Tap any card below for the fuller story of a character.

रामायण
Ramayana

The journey of Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana through exile, loss, and return — a meditation on duty within family and kingdom.

महाभारत
Mahabharata

The great war of Kurukshetra between the Pandavas and Kauravas, framing the Bhagavad Gita — Krishna's counsel to Arjuna on duty and action.

Characters of the Ramayana

Rama, Sita, and the figures whose choices carry the epic forward — tap any card for the fuller story.

राम
Rama
रामायण · Ramayana

The seventh avatar of Vishnu, Prince of Ayodhya, and the Ramayana's central figure — held across the tradition as maryada purushottama, the ideal man who never wavers from dharma regardless of personal cost.

सीता
Sita
रामायण · Ramayana

Rama's wife, held as an avatar of Lakshmi, and the Ramayana's central figure of steadfastness through exile, abduction, and a trial the epic never lets her escape entirely.

लक्ष्मण
Lakshmana
रामायण · Ramayana

Rama's younger brother, who left his own bride and the comforts of Ayodhya to accompany Rama and Sita into fourteen years of forest exile.

हनुमान
Hanuman
रामायण · Ramayana

The vanara devotee whose leap to Lanka, discovery of Sita, and burning of Ravana's city make him one of the Ramayana's most beloved figures — already met at his own shrine in this site's Tirtha chapter.

रावण
Ravana
रामायण · Ramayana

The ten-headed demon king of Lanka whose abduction of Sita sets the Ramayana's war in motion — a formidable scholar and devotee of Shiva as much as the epic's antagonist.

भरत
Bharata
रामायण · Ramayana

Rama's younger brother, who refused the throne given to him and ruled Ayodhya as regent in Rama's name for the full fourteen years of his exile.

दशरथ
Dasharatha
रामायण · Ramayana

King of Ayodhya and Rama's father, whose promise to his wife Kaikeyi sets the Ramayana's central exile in motion, and whose grief at losing Rama tradition holds took his life.

कैकेयी
Kaikeyi
रामायण · Ramayana

Dasharatha's third wife and Bharata's mother, whose two boons, called in on her maid Manthara's urging, sent Rama into exile and set the Ramayana's plot in motion.

Characters of the Mahabharata

The Pandavas, the Kauravas, and Krishna at the center of it all — tap any card for the fuller story.

कृष्ण
Krishna
महाभारत · Mahabharata

The eighth avatar of Vishnu, and the Mahabharata's guiding presence — charioteer, counselor, and, in the Bhagavad Gita, the voice of the epic's central teaching on duty.

अर्जुन
Arjuna
महाभारत · Mahabharata

The third of the five Pandava brothers, and the Mahabharata's central warrior — the disciple to whom Krishna delivers the Bhagavad Gita's teaching at Kurukshetra's edge.

भीष्म
Bhishma
महाभारत · Mahabharata

The Kuru dynasty's grand patriarch, bound by a vow of lifelong celibacy, who fought for the Kauravas out of duty despite loving the Pandavas as his own grandsons.

कर्ण
Karna
महाभारत · Mahabharata

Kunti's firstborn son, given away at birth and raised by a charioteer, whose loyalty to Duryodhana against his own unknown brothers makes him one of the Mahabharata's most tragic figures.

द्रौपदी
Draupadi
महाभारत · Mahabharata

Wife to all five Pandava brothers, born from a sacrificial fire, and the central figure whose public humiliation in the Kaurava court becomes the moral turning point that makes war unavoidable.

युधिष्ठिर
Yudhishthira
महाभारत · Mahabharata

The eldest Pandava, son of the god Dharma himself, whose devotion to truth and duty is tested as severely as it is upheld across the Mahabharata.

दुर्योधन
Duryodhana
महाभारत · Mahabharata

The eldest Kaurava, whose refusal to grant the Pandavas even five villages makes him the Mahabharata's chief antagonist and the war's proximate cause.

विस्तार से पढ़ें · Read more →
द्रोण
Drona
महाभारत · Mahabharata

The martial teacher (guru) to both the Pandavas and the Kauravas, whose own sense of duty as a warrior ultimately outweighs his affection for his most gifted student.
